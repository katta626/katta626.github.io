DART: transient pipeline and web integration

2025 to now · Radio Astronomy Lab, Raman Research Institute

The Gauribidanur Diamond Array Radio Telescope is an array of 64 log-periodic dipole antennas that observes bright pulsars and solar transients between 130 and 350 MHz. To catch millisecond bursts as they happen, I built its FRB-processing pipeline: converting raw ADC samples into filterbank data, processing the radio time series automatically, and spreading the work across processes in parallel. I also worked on the digital receiver backend, and I am moving interference removal and transient detection onto the GPU so that candidates are found in real time. The pipeline code is integrated with a web page.

Paper: Time-Domain Astronomy using Diamond Array Radio Telescope (DART), IEEE Xplore, accepted August 2026.

Dispersion and dedispersion

On its way to us a radio pulse crosses thin ionised gas between the stars. Lower frequencies travel slower, so the pulse arrives as a sweep: high frequencies first, low ones later. The delay grows with the dispersion measure, DM, the column of free electrons along the line of sight.

Δt≈4.15 ms×DM× (flo−2−fhi−2)

DM in pc cm−3, frequencies in GHz.

Shift each channel back by its delay and sum: the pulse snaps into a sharp peak. Get the DM wrong and it smears into the noise. Searching over trial DMs is how pulsars and fast radio bursts are found.

A two-element interferometer for solar weather

May to July 2023 · Gauribidanur Radio Observatory

Before DART, I helped develop and commission a two-element broadband interferometer of log-periodic dipole antennas in the SKA low-frequency band, with a web tool to acquire and analyse its data remotely. I observed the Sun and the Galaxy with it for a month. It has since recorded solar radio bursts of every type for about three years, supporting low-frequency solar weather monitoring.

Paper: Innovative Web Tool for Remote Data Acquisition and Analysis, Journal of Astrophysics and Astronomy 46, 26, 2025.
